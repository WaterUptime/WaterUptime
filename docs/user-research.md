# User research

> **Status: not started.** No interviews have been conducted. This document is the plan, the method and the place where anonymised findings will be logged.

The goal of discovery is to decide [Gate A](../ROADMAP.md#m2-discovery--gate-a-problem-fit): **is there a meaningful, repeated problem in how water-point repairs are coordinated and verified, in a specific place?** A "no" is a useful result.

## Who to talk to

Aim for **8–10 conversations**, with at least one from each group:

| Group | Why |
|---|---|
| Community water committees or local monitors | Experience faults first; report them; live with delays |
| Repair technicians or local service providers | Do the work; know what evidence is asked for and what gets disputed |
| LGA WASH staff | Usually triage, assign and own the assets |
| A WASH NGO or implementing partner | Run programmes across communities; often fund maintenance |
| A funder or programme manager (if possible) | Need to reconcile spending with verified work |

Narrow to **one locality** as early as possible. Problems and systems differ between places.

## Questions we need answered

1. How is a fault reported today, and where is it recorded?
2. Who decides whether a fault is valid, and how is it prioritised?
3. Who assigns the technician, and who pays for the work?
4. What evidence is required to mark a repair complete?
5. Who confirms that water service is restored, and are they independent?
6. How are repeat failures, disputes, fraud allegations and corrections handled?
7. Which digital systems are already used (e.g. WASHIMS, spreadsheets, messaging apps)? Who owns the data?
8. Which step is slowest, most costly, hardest to audit, or most often fails?
9. Would a record that several parties share, and nobody can quietly edit, solve that, or would a better-run database do?
10. What would make the proposed workflow unusable or unsafe for community members?

## Interview guide (≈45 minutes)

**Introduction (5 min).** Who we are; that WaterUptime is an early prototype with no government or NGO partnership; that we're learning, not selling. Go through consent.

**Their role (5 min).** What do you do when a water point breaks? Who do you work with?

**Last real fault (15 min).** Walk me through the most recent breakdown you were involved in:
- How did you find out? Who did you tell, and how?
- What happened next? How long did each step take?
- How did everyone know it was fixed? Who checked?
- Was anything written down? Where? Who can see it now?
- Did anything go wrong or cause disagreement?

**Pain points (10 min).** Which part of that process frustrates you most? What do you do about it today? Have you seen a repair recorded as done when it wasn't?

**Tools and data (5 min).** What phone or software do you use for this? Who owns the records? What happens to them later?

**Concept reaction (5 min, optional, only at the end).** Show the demo workflow with synthetic data. Would this help, get in the way, or make no difference? Who would need to agree to use it?

Ask about **past behaviour**, not hypotheticals. Don't pitch before the last section.

## Consent and data handling

- Explain the purpose, that participation is voluntary, and that people can stop at any time.
- Get consent before recording. Notes are fine without recording.
- **Don't collect** names of community members, phone numbers, exact locations or anything about households unless it's essential and agreed.
- Store raw notes privately, not in this repository. Delete recordings once they're transcribed and anonymised.
- Anonymise before anything is shared: use role and locality type only (e.g. *"Technician, peri-urban LGA"*).
- Don't promise benefits, payment or follow-up you can't deliver.
- Where institutions are involved, get their permission before interviewing staff in an official capacity.

## Synthesis

After each conversation, record within 24 hours:

- **Process map:** steps, actors, tools and durations for the fault they described
- **Pain points:** quote (anonymised) + frequency + severity
- **Systems:** tools in use, data owner, access
- **Surprises:** anything that contradicts our hypothesis

After all conversations, cluster pain points across participants. A finding counts toward Gate A only if it is **raised independently by several participants, or backed by records**.
